# Rheseus Factor Resolution

Read the [review standard](../../../docs/REVIEW_STANDARD.md). Use the supplied
analysis, agreements and approved positions as the record; identify unsupported
new propositions instead of silently introducing them during compression.

Prioritize what changes the decision. For each material factor, state the
resolution directly, followed by a short reason or condition only when needed.
Use the user's requested format. A useful default is:

**Factor → Resolution.** Add the fallback or evidence condition if it changes
whether the recommendation can be acted on.

Keep existing contractual rights, proposed changes and legal requirements
distinct. Proposed amounts, periods, percentages and commercial defaults must
remain labeled as proposals. A missing document is an evidence gap, not proof
that the associated right is absent. Do not turn a conditional recommendation
into clearance by shortening it.

If an email is requested, match the supplied voice examples and audience. In
the absence of a length instruction, aim for roughly 100–150 words: a clear
recommendation, two or three reasons or conditions, and the specific next
action. Use restrained rhetoric, ordinary words and short paragraphs. Do not
invent a prior call, agreement, attachment, recipient name or sender credential.
Deliver the draft; sending it requires the user's authorization.

Use source anchors in the brief or accompanying record where they help verify
the recommendation. Avoid loading a short client email with technical
citations unless the audience needs them. Keep every material blocking issue
visible in the brief, even when several can share one bullet.

If a diagram clarifies control, money, IP or patient/data flows, show the actual
or proposed arrangement with its status labeled. Omit decorative diagrams and
do not imply that a flowchart establishes legal compliance.
